Growth Through Connection

You are supported at every stage of the journey.

Friendship Speaker’s Circle uses a layered mentoring system so members receive support from within the club while also growing into the broader mentoring opportunities available through Toastmasters.

Our Mentor Ecosystem

Three layers of support.

Each layer serves a different purpose. Members should know who supports their club experience, who supports their individual growth, and how Toastmasters mentoring connects to Pathways.

Layer 1 — Club Development

Club Mentor

The Club Mentor supports Friendship Speaker’s Circle as an organization—helping strengthen club quality, leadership practices, member experience, and long-term sustainability.

Club Mentor: Kimbaley
Layer 2 — Member Development

Board Member Mentors

Board members serve as direct mentors to club members. They help members navigate onboarding, meeting roles, speech preparation, participation, goal-setting, and connection to club resources.

Layer 3 — Toastmasters Development

Pathways & Toastmasters Mentoring

Members are introduced to Toastmasters mentoring in Level 2 of Pathways. After Level 2, eligible members can explore the optional Pathways Mentor Program and broader Toastmasters mentoring opportunities.

Who Does What?

Clear roles prevent members from getting lost.

Kimbaley — Club Mentor

  • Supports the club’s overall development.
  • Advises club leadership and officers.
  • Helps strengthen meeting quality and club systems.
  • Provides an experienced Toastmasters perspective.
  • Supports the club as it grows toward sustainability.

Board Member Mentors

  • Maintain direct contact with assigned members.
  • Help members understand meeting roles.
  • Encourage speech and Pathways progress.
  • Share relevant resources and opportunities.
  • Notice when a member feels disconnected and reconnect them.

Members / Protégés

  • Communicate goals and questions honestly.
  • Respond to mentor outreach.
  • Prepare for roles and speeches.
  • Ask for specific feedback.
  • Take ownership of their own growth.
Pathways Connection

Mentoring becomes part of the learning experience.

Mentorship is not separate from member development. Toastmasters formally introduces mentoring in Level 2, and members who complete Level 2 may choose to continue into the Pathways Mentor Program.

Official Toastmasters program requirements may change. Members should use Toastmasters International and Base Camp as the source of truth for current requirements.

Mentoring Rhythm

What ongoing support can look like.

Mentoring should be consistent enough to matter without becoming another heavy obligation.

At Onboarding

Connect

Learn the member’s goals, explain club expectations, and identify the first small step.

Before Meetings

Prepare

Check whether the member has a role or speech and make sure they know where to find resources.

Monthly

Check In

Discuss participation, Pathways progress, challenges, and one next action.

At Milestones

Celebrate

Recognize completed speeches, roles, levels, leadership growth, and increased confidence.

Mentor Conversations

Questions that move members forward.

What are you working on right now?Identify the communication or leadership skill the member most wants to strengthen.
What have you done since our last conversation?Celebrate action before jumping immediately to what is incomplete.
What is getting in the way?Identify confusion, scheduling, nerves, resource gaps, or other barriers.
What is your next meeting role?Use roles intentionally instead of treating sign-up as a random assignment.
What feedback are you trying to apply?Connect evaluations to intentional practice.
What is one action you will take next?End every check-in with a concrete next step.
When a Member Goes Quiet

Reconnect before assuming disengagement.

Mentorship is especially valuable when someone begins to disappear from meetings or stops participating.

1

Reach Out Personally

Send a supportive message that focuses on the person—not just attendance or an unfinished task.

2

Reduce the Barrier

Offer a manageable next step: attend, take a small role, meet for 15 minutes, or review one resource.

3

Reconnect to Their Why

Remind the member what they said they wanted to gain and help them choose a realistic way back in.

The Goal of Mentoring

Support people until they can support themselves—and eventually support someone else.

A strong mentoring culture creates confident members, stronger leaders, and a club that does not depend on one person to keep everyone moving.
