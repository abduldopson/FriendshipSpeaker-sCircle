Meeting Flow

Who has control—and what happens next.

The person with control formally introduces the next person. When a role ends, control is clearly returned rather than assumed.

1

Sergeant at Arms

Calls the meeting to order, welcomes members and guests, reviews basic etiquette, and introduces the President.

Handoff: “Please welcome our Club President, [Name].”
2

Club President

Welcomes the club, gives only essential announcements or recognition, and introduces the Toastmaster of the Day.

Handoff: “Please welcome tonight’s Toastmaster of the Day, [Name].”
3

Toastmaster of the Day

Introduces the theme, reviews the agenda, explains the meeting experience for guests, and introduces the General Evaluator.

Handoff: Introduce the General Evaluator by name and role.
4

General Evaluator

Introduces the evaluation team: Speech Evaluator(s), Timer, Grammarian, and Ah-Counter. The Grammarian gives the Word of the Day.

Handoff: Returns control clearly to the Toastmaster of the Day.
5

Prepared Speeches

The Toastmaster introduces each speaker with name, title, project/objective when appropriate, and time requirement.

Handoff: Thank the speaker, allow feedback time when used, then introduce the next segment.
6

Table Topics

The Topics Master explains the purpose and timing, asks the question before naming a participant, and manages impromptu speaking.

Handoff: Returns control to the Toastmaster of the Day.
7

Evaluation Session

The General Evaluator leads speech evaluations, Timer, Grammarian, Ah-Counter, and the overall meeting evaluation.

Handoff: Returns control to the Toastmaster of the Day.
8

Toastmaster Wrap-Up

Connects back to the theme, thanks speakers and role holders, and completes the educational program.

Handoff: Returns control to the President.
9

President Closing

Recognizes guests, shares awards or acknowledgments, confirms next steps and upcoming dates, and prepares the room for adjournment.

Handoff: Turns the meeting over to the Sergeant at Arms.
10

Sergeant at Arms

Formally adjourns the meeting and supports room reset or virtual closeout.

Close: “This meeting of Friendship Speaker’s Circle is officially adjourned.”

Professional Transitions

What a strong handoff sounds like.

You do not need a long script. One clear sentence is enough to keep the room organized and make the next person feel prepared.

SAA → President

Opening handoff

“Please welcome our Club President, [Name].”
President → Toastmaster

Program handoff

“To lead us through tonight’s program, please welcome our Toastmaster of the Day, [Name].”
Toastmaster → General Evaluator

Evaluation team setup

“To introduce tonight’s evaluation team, please welcome our General Evaluator, [Name].”
Topics Master → Toastmaster

Return control

“Thank you to everyone who participated. I now return control to our Toastmaster of the Day, [Name].”
General Evaluator → Toastmaster

Close evaluation

“That concludes the evaluation portion of our meeting. I return control to our Toastmaster of the Day.”
Toastmaster → President

Return club control

“Thank you for an engaging meeting. I now return control to our Club President, [Name].”
Meeting Readiness

Before, during, and after.

A great meeting is not only what happens between 6:00 and 7:00. Preparation and follow-through matter too.

Before

Prepare the Room

  • Confirm all roles and speakers.
  • Verify speech titles and time limits.
  • Confirm the Word of the Day.
  • Check room or virtual setup.
  • Have the agenda ready before start time.
During

Protect the Experience

  • Use formal handoffs.
  • Keep explanations concise.
  • Support every speaker.
  • Protect timing.
  • Make guests feel oriented and welcome.
After

Close the Loop

  • Record attendance and roles.
  • Document awards or milestones.
  • Confirm upcoming role gaps.
  • Capture one meeting improvement.
  • Reset the room or close the virtual space.
Golden Rules

The standards every member should know.

These are not just officer expectations. They are shared habits that protect the quality of the meeting.

1No awkward handoffs.
Introduce the next person by name and role. Never simply say “next.”
2Someone always has control.
At every moment, members should know who owns the floor.
3Start and end on time.
Do not punish prepared members because someone else arrived late.
4Prepare before the meeting.
Role holders should know responsibilities, timing, names, and order before call to order.
5Applaud every speaker.
Prepared speeches, Table Topics, evaluations, and major roles deserve visible support.
6Keep transitions brief.
The Toastmaster connects the program but does not compete with the speakers.
7Ask the Table Topics question first.
Then name the participant so everyone listens to the prompt.
8Feedback builds people.
Be specific, constructive, respectful, and focused on improvement.
9Guests should understand the room.
Explain Toastmasters language and traditions without overexplaining.
10Leave each meeting better.
Identify one improvement and apply it at the next meeting.
