Who Is This For?

You do not need to think of yourself as a “public speaker.”

The club is for people who want to communicate more effectively in real professional and personal situations.

🗣️Emerging SpeakersYou want to become more comfortable speaking in front of others.
💼ProfessionalsYou want stronger presentations, meetings, interviews, and workplace communication.
🌱Growing LeadersYou want practice facilitating, mentoring, giving feedback, and leading teams.
🚀Experienced CommunicatorsYou want a structured place to keep sharpening your craft and help others grow.
What We Ask of Members

The club works when members participate.

Membership is not about perfection. It is about showing up, practicing, contributing, and helping create a supportive room for others.

Member Commitments

  • Attend meetings consistently when possible.
  • Take meeting roles and prepare for them.
  • Work toward speeches and Pathways goals.
  • Give constructive and respectful feedback.
  • Communicate when support is needed.
  • Celebrate and encourage other members.

What Members Can Expect From the Club

  • A welcoming and respectful environment.
  • Clear meeting systems and role expectations.
  • Mentoring and access to support.
  • Useful resources for speeches and roles.
  • Specific feedback focused on growth.
  • Recognition for progress, effort, and contribution.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions guests often have.

Do I have to speak at my first meeting?

No. Guests can observe and learn how the meeting works. You may be invited to participate in Table Topics, but the goal is to make you feel welcomed—not pressured.

What if public speaking makes me nervous?

That is one of the most common reasons people join. The club gives you repeated, manageable opportunities to practice in a supportive environment.

Do I need previous Toastmasters experience?

No. New members receive onboarding, meeting-role resources, mentoring, and guidance as they learn the Toastmasters experience.

How do I know what role to take?

Use the Meeting Roles page and shared Role Resource Drive. Your mentor or another club leader can also help you choose a good first role.

How do speeches work?

Members use Toastmasters Pathways projects and club resources to prepare speeches with specific objectives and time limits. Feedback helps members improve over time.

Where do I find club resources after joining?

The Member Hub and Resources Library organize role sign-up, attendance, awards, Pathways resources, meeting guides, mentoring, and shared Google Drive materials.
