New Member Roadmap

Your first month should feel clear—not confusing.

Use this page as your step-by-step guide to getting connected, taking your first meeting role, beginning your Toastmasters learning journey, and preparing your Ice Breaker speech.

First 30 Days

Eight steps. One clear beginning.

You do not have to master Toastmasters before participating. Your goal is to begin, practice, get feedback, and build confidence over time.

1

Meet Your Mentor

Share why you joined, what you want to improve, and how often you would like to check in.

2

Complete Club Onboarding

Finish required membership forms, profile information, and club commitment materials.

3

Access Toastmasters Resources

Make sure you can access the Toastmasters platform and any official education materials assigned to you.

4

Understand the Meeting Flow

Learn how the SAA, President, Toastmaster, speakers, Table Topics, and evaluation team work together.

5

Take Your First Meeting Role

Start with a role that helps you observe the meeting closely—Timer, Ah-Counter, or Grammarian are great first choices.

6

Choose Your First Speech Project

Review your official Toastmasters education materials and identify the project you will complete first.

7

Prepare Your Ice Breaker

Introduce yourself through a meaningful story, experience, value, or turning point that helps the club know you better.

8

Track Your Growth

Record roles, speeches, feedback, milestones, and one communication goal you are intentionally working on.

New Member Quick Check

Before your first month ends, can you say yes to these?

✓
I know my mentor.
I know how to contact them.
✓
I understand the meeting flow.
I know who controls each section.
✓
I have taken a role.
I practiced contributing before giving a speech.
✓
I know my next speech.
I understand its purpose and time requirement.
✓
I have one growth goal.
I know what I want to improve next.
A Suggested Pace

Your first four weeks.

This is a guide, not a race. Members can move faster or slower depending on comfort, scheduling, and access to resources.

Week 1

Connect

Meet your mentor, complete onboarding, and observe the meeting structure.

Week 2

Participate

Take a small meeting role and practice your first formal handoff.

Week 3

Prepare

Outline and rehearse your first prepared speech with mentor support.

Week 4

Speak & Reflect

Deliver your speech or schedule it, review feedback, and choose your next goal.

Club Expectations

What we ask of every member.

You do not need perfection. You do need participation, preparation, respect, and a willingness to grow.

Be present.Attend consistently enough to build momentum and relationships.
Take roles.Meetings work because members contribute—not because a few people do everything.
Prepare.Know your role or speech requirements before the meeting begins.
Give useful feedback.Be specific, respectful, encouraging, and focused on growth.
Communicate.If you cannot fulfill a role or commitment, let the appropriate person know early.
Support the room.Listen, applaud, encourage, and help create a psychologically safe learning environment.
What Success Looks Like

You are progressing when...

  • You speak even when you are nervous.
  • You can identify one thing you improved.
  • You can name one thing you want to practice next.
  • You are taking increasingly challenging roles.
  • You are helping another member feel supported.
Your Next Move

Choose a meeting role, review the meeting flow, connect with your mentor, or begin planning your first speech.
